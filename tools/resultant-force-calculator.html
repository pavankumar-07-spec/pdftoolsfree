What is Resultant Force?

A resultant force is a single vector force that produces the exact same mechanical effect on a body as two or more concurrent forces combined. Finding the resultant force is one of the most fundamental tasks in Engineering Mechanics and Physics.

Resultant Force Formulas

When two forces $F_1$ and $F_2$ act at a point with an included angle $\theta$ between them, the resultant magnitude $R$ and its angle $\alpha$ relative to force $F_1$ are calculated using the Parallelogram Law of Forces:

  • Magnitude Formula: $R = \sqrt{F_1^2 + F_2^2 + 2 \cdot F_1 \cdot F_2 \cdot \cos\theta}$
  • Direction Angle Formula: $\alpha = \arctan\left(\frac{F_2 \sin\theta}{F_1 + F_2 \cos\theta}\right)$
  • Cartesian Component Method: $\Sigma F_x = F_1 + F_2 \cos\theta, \quad \Sigma F_y = F_2 \sin\theta \implies R = \sqrt{(\Sigma F_x)^2 + (\Sigma F_y)^2}$

Solved Engineering Example

Problem Statement:

Two structural cables exert forces $F_1 = 50\,\text{N}$ and $F_2 = 80\,\text{N}$ at a eye-bolt anchor. The angle between the two cables is $\theta = 60^\circ$. Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant force.

Step 1: Calculate Magnitude:
$R = \sqrt{50^2 + 80^2 + 2(50)(80)\cos(60^\circ)} = \sqrt{2500 + 6400 + 4000} = \sqrt{12900} \approx 113.58\,\text{N}$

Step 2: Calculate Direction Angle $\alpha$:
$\alpha = \arctan\left(\frac{80 \sin 60^\circ}{50 + 80 \cos 60^\circ}\right) = \arctan\left(\frac{69.28}{90}\right) \approx 37.59^\circ$

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Degree vs Radian Errors: Entering angle $\theta$ in degrees into JavaScript `Math.cos()` without converting to radians ($\text{rad} = \theta \times \frac{\pi}{180}$).
  • Scalar Addition Mismatch: Adding force magnitudes directly ($50 + 80 = 130\,\text{N}$) instead of taking directional vector components into account.
