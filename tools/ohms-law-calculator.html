What is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's Law is the fundamental principle governing electrical circuits. Formulated by German physicist Georg Simon Ohm in 1827, it states that the electrical current ($I$) flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage ($V$) applied across it and inversely proportional to its resistance ($R$).

Ohm's Law Formulas

Depending on which two variables are known, you can calculate the remaining parameters using these variations:

  • Voltage (V): $V = I \times R$   (Volts = Amps × Ohms)
  • Current (I): $I = \frac{V}{R}$   (Amps = Volts / Ohms)
  • Resistance (R): $R = \frac{V}{I}$   (Ohms = Volts / Amps)
  • Power (P): $P = V \times I = I^2 \times R = \frac{V^2}{R}$   (Watts)

Solved Textbook Example

Example Problem:

An electric heating element with a resistance of $24\,\Omega$ is connected to a $120\,\text{V}$ power source. Calculate the current drawn and the power dissipated by the heater.

Given: $V = 120\,\text{V}$, $R = 24\,\Omega$

Current Calculation: $I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{120}{24} = 5\,\text{A}$

Power Calculation: $P = V \times I = 120 \times 5 = 600\,\text{W}$

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Mixing Prefix Units: Forgetting to convert milliamperes ($\text{mA}$) into Amperes ($\text{A}$) before calculating ($100\,\text{mA} = 0.1\,\text{A}$).
  • Applying to Non-Ohmic Devices: Trying to use Ohm's Law directly on non-linear components like LEDs or semiconductor diodes.
  • Ignoring Temperature Effects: Resistor values drift as they heat up during operation, altering the ideal linear behavior.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Ohm's Law apply to AC circuits?

Yes, for purely resistive AC circuits. In reactive AC circuits with inductors or capacitors, resistance $R$ is replaced by impedance $Z$: $V = I \times Z$.

What happens if resistance is zero?

If $R=0\,\Omega$, current $I$ theoretically approaches infinity, creating a short circuit.
